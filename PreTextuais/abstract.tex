% Abstract (English).

Safe autonomous flight in low-altitude urban airspace requires an aircraft to
build, on board and in real time, a calibrated picture of the obstacles around
it. This dissertation develops the perception (\emph{Sense}) layer of urban UAV
detect-and-avoid, structured as a single modular camera--point-cloud pipeline in
which a convolutional detector seeds a viewing frustum, a point-cloud network
lifts each detection into a metric three-dimensional box, and a recursive
Bayesian filter turns the stream of detections into calibrated tracks. The
pipeline is validated through three self-contained studies. First, the tracking
stage is formulated for a moving platform in global and local reference frames;
the two formulations are proven mathematically equivalent under a known platform
pose and isotropic process noise, their divergence regimes are characterized, and
the filter is shown to be near-optimal against the posterior Cram\'er--Rao bound.
Second, the same estimator is taken to real flights, tracking a person from a
monocular UAV whose pose is estimated rather than known, where propagating the
camera-pose uncertainty into the innovation is shown to be necessary for
statistical consistency. Third, a purpose-built synthetic AirSim dataset trains
the detection front-end, and an empirical comparison identifies foreground
extraction, rather than the box regressor, as the dominant lever on
three-dimensional localization accuracy. Together, the studies show that one
recursive estimator, properly instrumented with its uncertainty, serves the Sense
stage across sensors, targets, and environments.
